Issue #219: validated local implementation for review

At 20,000 singleton genes x 100 samples, median donor preparation fell from
39.92 s to 0.08 s (about 499x); end-to-end Mapping fell from 40.95 s to 0.32 s
(about 128x). All full-size donor/exclusion and public Mapping comparisons were
identical(), and the scientific/diagnostic regression gates passed.

The improvement is not uniform: 5,000-row duplicate-heavy preparation had a
slower median, 5.04 s to 5.79 s (about 15% slower), with mixed paired results.
No improvement is claimed for that case. Duplicate-heavy Mapping and 20,000-row
duplicate-heavy preparation showed modest gains in this run, not a universal
speed guarantee.

Environment, method and reproduction
------------------------------------
Windows 11 x64 build 26200; R 4.6.2 (2026-10-29 ucrt); LC_ALL=C. Intel Core
i9-11950H (8 physical/16 logical cores), 68,425,691,136 bytes RAM.
Synthetic rnorm(mean=5, sd=1), seed=219+row_count; 100 samples, method=max,
RawCounts=FALSE, impute=TRUE, verbose=FALSE. Entrez 596 is the sole represented
shared NC/SSP target; sample 50 is missing. All background donors are eligible.
Singleton-heavy means 100% singleton groups (5,000/20,000 Entrez genes).
Duplicate-heavy means 90% of feature rows in pairs, 10% singletons, giving
2,750/11,000 Entrez groups in 5,000/20,000 feature rows. The target is a singleton.

The baseline donor function is loaded from the pinned commit; the candidate is
loaded from the checkout. All other code, data, frozen estimator and inputs are
shared. Exact donor/Mapping gates warm each implementation. The estimator is
warmed separately. Timings alternate B/C, C/B, B/C with gc() before each call;
profiling/allocation capture runs separately. Other desktop activity was not
controlled, including a pre-existing unrelated R process that was left untouched.
